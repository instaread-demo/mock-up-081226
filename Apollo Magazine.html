US appeals court blocks Trump’s ballroom

By Apollo, 9 August 2026

A federal appeals court in the District of Columbia has upheld a ruling halting construction on the ballroom on the site of what used to be the East Wing of the White House. The original injunction was granted on the grounds that the president has no authority to demolish or build on the site without authorisation from Congress – and the two appeal judges expressing the majority opinion agreed: ‘Whether or not a ⁠massive ballroom should be constructed is for Congress to decide and is not a matter for Executive self-help,’ they wrote in their decision. The court has delayed implementation of its ruling to allow the Trump administration to appeal to the Supreme Court – a move that seems likely after President Trump describing it on social media as a ‘horrendous’ and ‘politically motivated’ decision.

A Russian airstrike destroyed a 300-year-old Cossack church in southern Ukraine on 2 August, the Kyiv Post reports. Made entirely out of wood without using a single metal nail, the Holy Vvedenska Church had been dismantled and rebuilt several times, most recently in the historic site of Beryslav in the Kherson region. Oleksandr Alchiev, head of the Beryslav City Military Administration, described the church, which had a bright blue and green exterior, as an ‘architectural monument of national significance’ on Facebook. MOCT reports that at the time of attack the church was under the administration of the Moscow Patriarchate, aka the Russian Orthodox Church. In 2023 Beryslav’s military administration appealed to Ukraine’s ministry of culture to return the church to state ownership but received no response.

The Trump administration has proposed cutting the Smithsonian Institution’s federal funding by 11 per cent, which would leave it with its lowest budget in almost a decade, ARTnews reports. The proposed budget for the fiscal year 2027 is $961.3m in federal funding, down from the $1bn granted in 2026. The government also proposes cutting the Smithsonian’s workforce by six per cent – some 243 employees – as well as reducing its salaries and expenses account by $87.2m and facilities spending by $32m. The proposal is the latest in the Trump administration’s attacks on the Smithsonian Institution, which it has accused of ‘extreme political activism’ and most recently of containing ‘inaccurate information’ in its displays.
