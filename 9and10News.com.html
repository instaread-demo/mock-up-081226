Mesick, Benzie schools to re-submit funding requests after August rejections

Sheldon Krause
08-11-2026 at 06:40:38 PM EDT
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by

Instaread

TRAVERSE CITY — Two Northern Michigan school districts are making funding requests to voters this November after their proposals were turned down last week.

The requests cover school renovations for one district and everyday operations for the other.

Both Mesick Consolidated Schools and Benzie Central Schools had funding requests rejected by voters in last the Aug. 4 election.

Advertisement

Mesick’s request was for $19.3 million that would cover renovations to their 1966 elementary school building.

“Completely go through and put new HVAC and roofing and all the systems from within,” said Mesick Superintendent Jack Ledford. “Design the classrooms for our elementary students, K-5.”

That proposal has been scaled back to $17.1 million, with the district dropping plans to demolish some unused parts of the building and relocating the school’s playground.

“The district will figure out how to how to take care of that in the meantime — but we felt like scaling that back was the necessary thing to do for November,” Ledford said.

Advertisement

Benzie’s requests were for their operating millage and sinking fund.

The operating millage is required for the district to receive its annual funding from the state, while the sinking fund goes to improvements like building renovations and bus services.

Benzie Superintendent Amiee Erfourth says that the financial impact this year would be more than $60,000 and close to $400,000 next year.

“This year we’re already looking at limiting overtime and maybe not purchasing things that we had planned to purchase — curriculum, different things,” she said. “But next year we wouldn’t have the ability to cover that in our fund balance. So it would be a bigger impact next year, and potentially could be cutting programs or staffing.”

Advertisement

Benzie’s operating millage only is charged to non-primary residences, like second homes, vacant lots or businesses.

The sinking fund would also provide a reduction in the amount paid by property owners, but the district needs re-approval to expand the fund’s scope.

Erfourth says that the loss of funding could result in job losses the district, which is the second largest employer in Benzie County.

“I’ve heard people saying concern about, ‘I don’t have kids in school anymore. Why should I support this?’ But your neighbor might be employed here. There might be people in your community that are employed here, so it could be a financial impact to the greater community as well,” she said.

The deadline for local officials to approve November ballot items was Tuesday at 4 p.m., leading to the tight turnaround after last week’s requests failed.
