The in-Cumbria Business Awards 2026 has its official host: A comedian and writer who has spent 20 years touring the world and appearing on the likes of Taskmaster, QI, and Have I Got News for You. Ahead of his appearance at The Halston in November, Mark Watson talks to Gareth Lloyd about how comedy and business intersect, and the secret to steering a successful corporate gig.

Build relationships. Listen to your audience. Adapt and evolve.

These are all mantras you can find business leaders sharing with budding entrepreneurs in seminars and workshops. But the man who is repeating them to me isn’t a member of the typical C-suite at all. He’s a comedian.

Mark Watson has climbed a very different kind of ladder to most – moving from cellar bars to arenas rather than from desk booths to boardrooms. But the lessons he’s learned along the way appear to be remarkably similar to the ones you hear in business. This is perhaps what makes him such a fitting host for the in-Cumbria Business Awards.

‘You have to be persistent to get anywhere in comedy – just like in business,’ Watson explains.

‘You have to ride out ups and downs. Any job where you're self-employed requires a lot of drive and determination.

‘Sometimes things go against you, you can be passed over for jobs. The entertainment world is pretty fickle, so you do have to have belief in what you're doing and stamina to keep going.

‘Interestingly, business overlaps with comedy all the time. You never know which relationships are going to be key. That's why it's important to give every show your best. You never know who's in the audience. Many comedians have had someone say, ‘I saw you five years ago in York’, and that turned out to be more important than it seemed at the time.

‘You should always treat every audience with the same respect but also remember you don’t know who might be there or what it could lead to.’

Mark Watson (Image: Supplied)

Watson didn’t learn all of this overnight. The comedian has been touring the world since ‘dipping his toe in the water’ at university, gradually working his way up through the open mic nights and onto the main circuit, where he’s been a fixture of the UK scene for over two decades.

Since his first gig in 2002, he has continued to rack up credits on stage and in television, including appearances on Taskmaster, Live at The Apollo, QI, Have I Got News for You, Would I Lie to You, and The Celebrity Island with Bear Grylls. He has also published eight novels, as well as presenting podcasts and radio shows.

It’s a career that has taken Watson right around the UK, across the seas, and back again. And he’s still touring widely today. Just a few weeks ago, he was playing in Montreal and Toronto, and this month he’s up in Scotland for the biggest festival for any comedian: Edinburgh Fringe (where he’ll be performing and producing shows).

Watson has played in Cumbria on several occasions during his career, including Carlisle, Kendal, Whitehaven and Barrow, but his visit to the region in November is the big one. At least, from where we’re sitting.

Watson will step out at The Halston in Carlisle on November 12 as the host for the in-Cumbria Business Awards, and whilst the ceremony is a few months off yet, he has – like any experienced comedian (or businessperson) – prepared a plan of action.

‘You have to plan quite a long way ahead in this career, often at least a year,’ he states.

‘It’s important to remember that corporate events are there to celebrate businesses or the local area: You as the comedian are not the primary attraction there, it's not all about you.

‘So, I try and tailor these events quite a bit. I'll always look into the businesses beforehand, take an interest in the event, chat to people. I like to make it personal to whoever it is that I'm performing for, because you're a guest in that circumstance.

‘Speed of delivery and efficiency matter. You have to bring a certain energy. I like to keep it fast, involve the audience, and comment on the event as we go along.

‘You don't want it to feel like everyone's just going through the motions. I try to react to what's in front of me and still make it as entertaining an evening as I can.’

Mark Watson (Image: Supplied)

Watson embraces an everyman style of comedy, reflecting on the messiness of life’s trials and tribulations whilst often making himself the butt of the joke.

It will be intriguing to see what he pulls out of the bag at the Awards, but we won’t know exactly what will materialise until the night in question. He may not know the whole script himself just yet.

Watson explains that every show is unique and is constantly being rewritten in his head right up until the moment he steps out on stage – embracing bursts of improvisation that happen organically during the performances.

‘Some things work immediately on stage, others are just the germ of an idea and it might be months before you get anywhere with it,’ he says.

‘You might come back to it later and realise what to do with it. It’s not an exact science. My approach is to hoard everything that might be useful and wait for the moment when you know what to do with it.’

Watson has also evolved the delivery of his act since starting out – moving into additional channels such as social media, podcasting and video.

‘The marketing of comedy and the way you attract an audience is much more about having an online presence and social media now,’ he says.

‘Those things are far more prominent than they were 20 years ago. There's more pressure to be active on all fronts at once, to be gigging, but also to be putting videos out there and be on socials.

‘TV was a very powerful way of getting yourself known when I started. It still is, but people don't watch it in the same way these days.

‘I was quite an early adopter of social media, but the prevalence of things like Instagram and TikTok and those video-based platforms have taken me a long time to adjust to, because I'm in my mid-40s, I don't spend much time on these sites myself. But now I do have to try.

‘I have had to embrace it. What's difficult is trying to make it sound organic and real, rather than as if you've just been forced to do it. You've all seen branded stuff which seems very forced and desperate, as if it's been designed to try and get as much attention as possible. It can ring very hollow if you’re not careful.’

Mark Watson (Image: Supplied)

Watson also compares his growth as a comedian to the kind that business leaders experience as the years pass by, explaining that priorities and areas of focus begin to shift.

‘When you’re starting out, you want to do every gig and chase every opportunity,’ he says.

‘There’s always competition, but as you get older you become less focused on that and more on your own life and who you want to be.

‘I’ve learned to balance work goals with life goals better. For me, being a touring comedian was one of the main things I wanted. I wanted to play festivals around the world, and I’ve done that.

‘There are still things I haven’t done. Everyone has things that stay out of reach. Over time, you learn to reflect on what you’ve achieved and the ways you’ve been fortunate, even if you haven’t done everything yet.

‘It’s important to have appreciation, because there are many people who would love to do this job.

‘If my younger self had seen where I am now, I think he’d have been very happy…’

The in-Cumbria Business Awards 2026 will take place at The Halston in Carlisle on Thursday, November 12.

Brought to you in partnership once again with Egremont-based NRL, one of the country's top technical and engineering recruitment businesses, the ceremony will celebrate the best in business right across the region.

The categories for the 2026 in-Cumbria Business Awards are:

BUSINESS LEADER OF THE YEAR

Sponsored by: Rachael Bell Wealth Management

A business leader who has made an outstanding contribution to their company in the last year, demonstrating exceptional commitment, inspirational leadership and vision.

APPRENTICE/NEWCOMER OF THE YEAR

An individual who has made an exceptional impact in their early career, whether as an apprentice, trainee, recent graduate or a young entrepreneur.

BUSINESS OF THE YEAR

Sponsored by: Cartmell Shepherd Solicitors

An exceptional business that has demonstrated outstanding performance, leadership and contributions in its industry.

SME BUSINESS OF THE YEAR

Sponsored by: David Allen

A business with one to 50 employees that can demonstrate strong leadership and vision, as well as good sales growth and sound financial performance.

NEW BUSINESS OF THE YEAR

Sponsored by: Fibrus Broadband

This award goes to a successful company that has been in operation for less than three years.

FAMILY BUSINESS OF THE YEAR

Sponsored by: Lamont Pridmore

A family-owned or family-managed business which has at least two members of a family involved in running it, and which can clearly demonstrate how it has achieved success.

BEST COMMUNITY INVOLVEMENT

Sponsored by: React Engineering

A business that can show its proactive commitment to putting something back into their local community.

FLYING THE FLAG FOR CUMBRIA AWARD

Sponsored by: University of Cumbria

A Cumbrian-headquartered business putting the county on the map with the products and services it offers.

MARKETING EXCELLENCE AWARD

Outstanding achievements in marketing strategy, creativity, and results.

 DIVERSITY AND INCLUSION AWARD

Sponsored by: The Cumberland

A business that has demonstrated a strong and ongoing commitment to fostering a diverse, inclusive, and equitable environment for all employees, customers, and stakeholders.

RETAIL/LEISURE AND HOSPITALITY BUSINESS OF THE YEAR

Sponsored by NatWest

A business in the retail, leisure, or hospitality sector that has demonstrated outstanding performance, innovation, and success - with exceptional commitment to customer satisfaction, operational excellence, and community engagement.

Two special awards will also be handed out on the night: The Lifetime Achievement Award sponsored by BAE; and the Nuclear Ambassador Award sponsored by NRL.

The evening will also kick off with a drinks reception sponsored by Furness Building Society.
